Priority map

Build the dominance relation between eligible applicants, then peel it into priority tiers.

How this stays fast as the list grows

Finding the top tier only compares a new applicant against the current leading group, not every applicant on file, so adding one more name doesn't mean redoing every past comparison. At the scale a single committee handles by hand, brute-force comparison would finish just as fast; the saving becomes visible once the list runs into the hundreds.

      What each stage is built on
      • Intake and eligibility rule runs on propositional logic (Module 1). The rule is parsed into an expression tree and reduced with rewrite rules — double negation, idempotent, complement, absorption, De Morgan's — instead of a truth table.
      • Dominance map is a relation and digraph (Module 3). Each applicant's outrank list is a singly linked list; together they form the adjacency list of a directed graph.
      • Priority tiers form a poset (Module 4). Tiers are the antichains left after repeatedly peeling off the maximal, non-dominated elements; the diagram drawn is the transitive reduction of the dominance relation, i.e. its Hasse diagram.
      • Review queue is a circular doubly linked list, so a reviewer can step forward or back through a tied tier and it wraps around instead of stopping at the end.
